Bring Your Older Routers Back From The Dead!

Want to get more life out of your old network routers with fancy new firmware? This post shows you the basics to get started as a router necromancer.

By Josh - Aug 05, 2026 | posts | software, networking, hardware

Maybe you can relate, a lightning storm barreled through my area the other day. It took out one of my routers and my home lab server. I had everything behind surge protectors, but I guess that didn’t matter.

After some recovery, I wanted a cheap fix. I took apart the router in an attempt to repair it, but one of the surface-mounted components had exploded, leaving bits of what looked like shrapnel and melted plastic around it.

So I switched back to an older router instead, a Netgear R7000 (AC1900). It handles just about anything I need for the time being, but it's also exceptionally outdated. The best move I could think of was to flash better firmware to it, so I considered DD-WRT as I have used it a bit in the past, but stumbled upon FreshTomato, which is not to be mistaken for AdvancedTomato, a similar product, but older.

Installing FreshTomato was easy, and the R7000 works great as a replacement for my last router, for now. It's also sparked the itch to try several more older routers because it's so much easier to install than I was expecting, coming from DD-WRT a really long time ago. Not that DD-WRT is bad, I just didn’t know what I was doing.

It also just feels weird to say that something from 2017 is “older”. Sigh.

WARNING: Flashing 3rd-party firmware can permanently break or damage any router.

Research Before Starting

Before starting, make sure the appropriate packages are even available to install. The main factor to focus on is the CPU and architecture, and other various versioning characters after the router model name on the sticker, usually on the back or bottom of virtually every router.

Example: R7000 VS R7000P.

In this case, they look like the same router, but the "P" is not supported.

This means YOUR router may not be supported or needs different files, EVEN if you have what looks like the SAME router, but with different specs, research thoroughly and double check the compatibility page.

R7000 (AC1900 Nighthawk)

Find The R7000 Firmware

I used these files for my R7000:

  • Initial files: here
  • All In One (AIO) Files: here

The files you need will be labeled with "R7000", you might have to scroll down to find them. Extract both initial and the AIO files to a location you will remember. Ignore any VPN files.

  • freshtomato-R7000-xxx-initial-##k, grab the .chk file
  • freshtomato-R7000-xxx-AIO-##k, grab the .trx file

I highly recommend validating the checksum, the values should be at the bottom of the download page you are using for your files and named "MD5SUM" and "SHA256SUM" or similar. If you don't know what I am talking about, read this.

Factory Reset

The install is a two step flashing process and recommends being on the original stock firmware to start.

So the first step is to factory reset the router by holding down the reset button on the back for roughly 20 seconds. This will erase whatever you have on the router, and if you bought this used, I personally wouldn't connect this to your network until it’s fully reset, and even then, still not to the internet until flashed with newer firmware, so be careful.

Hint: use a paperclip or a pen to reach the button, you will feel it click.

Ensure its not plugged into anything except power, and let the router fully boot (all the lights are on).

Hold down the reset button for roughly 20 seconds. Lights should start blinking or stuttering. Once you release the reset button, it should reboot on its own after another 20-ish seconds. If not, manually reboot it.

Reach The Update Page

Connect an Ethernet cable from one of the ports on the router to a PC (NOT the one labeled "Internet" or "WAN"), then login to the admin panel and ensure it's on the right version. To reach the router via a browser, try one of these:

  • http://192.168.1.1
  • http://www.routerlogin.net

Hint: If using Firefox or similar with "HTTPS Only Mode", it might fight you to reach the admin panel. Turn this mode off in settings, clear your cache, restart the browser, then ensure you are going to HTTP. Don't forget to turn "HTTPS Only Mode" back on after you are done.

Banish the mobile app recommendation page by clicking the option that says you don't have a valid mobile phone. Then follow the wizard/genie steps. And yes, that is a 2012 MacBook Pro running Linux.

As you go through the wizard, select the options to not auto-update, and that you want to configure the connection yourself because there is no internet connection. Other than that, the settings don't really matter because we are going to flash new firmware anyway.

(I think there is a way to bypass the wizard/genie, but it wouldn't let me)

The password and username will be erased and irrelevant after the flash/install, so unless you are paranoid, you don't have to worry about it.

When it says "No Ethernet Cable is plugged into the Router Internet Port" its talking about WAN, not your local cable. You are fine. Just breath...

Install FreshTomato

After ensuring the router was working, it was time for the update page. (If you waited until now, download the files and extract them)

Navigate to the update page on your router.

Home Dashboard --> ADVANCED --> Administration --> Router Update

Then click "Browse" and upload the extracted initial files. In my case, all I had to do is click "Upload", I did not click apply, but you might need to. If something pops up claiming you are reverting to an older version or some nonsense, just click confirm/continue.

The system should start doing some voodoo. DO NOT unplug anything, don't even think about the power button or the Ethernet cable while it's doing the voodoo. The initial flash is the "make it or break it" moment.

It may look like the browser has stalled and had a stroke while waiting for the router to come back online. After a few minutes, if nothing changes and the router lights have flickered from the reboot, wait until it appears stable, you should be able to move to the next step. I personally had to do an additional manual reboot again.

Now try to navigate to 192.168.1.1, and the default login is:

  • user: root
  • pass: admin

Once done, I was greeted with the FreshTomato UI. If you don't, there is most likely some left over stock firmware still cached somewhere.

Here are some trouble shooting steps to clear it out:

  • Turn off the router an wait 30sec with the power cable fully unplugged. Then wait 2-3 minutes with the power back on. Then plug the Ethernet back in.
  • Double check you don't have strict security settings, aggressive script or ad blockers, or "HTTPS Only Mode" on your browser again, then clear the cache. (don't forget to turn them back on when you are done)
  • Do another reset by holding the button in the back using the same process as before in the "Factory Reset" section. However, this time it should not go back to the original Netgear firmware. If it was flashed properly, it will rest to the initial file we just flashed.

Final Form Flash

But wait theirs more! That was the initial flash. Next, do the nearly same same thing as before but use he the AIO file and go through the new FreshTomato UI under Administration this time.

I recommend checking the clear NVRAM options if you have it. This will avoid the possible leftover cache issue from before.

After another reboot, the router should officially be on FreshTomato, it will look virtually the same after flashing the initial files but be ready to configure. GZ!

One last step, just because you are on a fresh copy of shiny open source software to revamp an old router does NOT mean you are invisible. Take all the normal precautions and steps, like a solid admin password and desired security settings, then reboot and ensure it persisted.

R7000 Results

Compared to the stock firmware, this was much easier to use and navigate, and while not perfect, it was overall significantly better. Something I was really excited about was the UI themes, some of them looked kinda cool, but the setting is not in a super obvious spot, in my opinion.

Administration --> Admin Access --> theme UI

Then I recommend scrolling down the options and using Advanced Dark. It looks super clean considering we are on an old router.

I also noticed there was a lot more access to network interfaces and configuration, which may have been available on the default Netgear as well, but I never noticed anything this advanced. I eventually drifted into the VLAN section, since I was told there really isn't a “guest network” setting in FreshTomato. I was informed that default guest networks are a lame retail consumer feature, so I did some digging through Reddit and FreshTomato’s documentation.

It was time consuming because I am not super knowledgeable about networking, but it was never difficult to set up a wireless VLAN. Sadly, with the new VLAN settings in FreshTomato, I noticed I was no longer getting gigabit speeds. After some research, it turns out that the additional internal routing settings prevented full gigabit throughput.

The general advice I gathered was to use device isolation instead of VLANs for older routers if you want the full bandwidth potential per connection instead of trying to create a hodgpoge guest network.

Maybe there is a better way, or this is just confusion on my part, but I was hoping for the speed and the VLAN. To be fair, I’m asking for way more than I thought I would get out of this project.

Anyways, the fact that I could just flash some new software to my old router at all, and it was clean and easy to use, was refreshing.

Lastly, I have to be honest, I attempted to flash the R7000P (AC2300) version first, then checked the specs/support page. AKA I’m a dumb arse and tried to flash the stronger version of the R7000, which is not supported.

It ended up being fine, but don't do that. If you see R7000 "P", its no bueno as of the time I am writing this post. Maybe its not possible, but I hope someone changes that.
