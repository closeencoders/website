My AI is Bigger Than Yours

Will we lose a generation of would-be experts to the belief that robots are 'good enough' at most tasks?

By Josh - Apr 18, 2026 | posts | ai, rant, sarcasm

People are not afraid to share their opinions on the internet, but they sure are afraid of falling behind others who do the same.

Yet, it seems like anything of value (opinion or not) released into the wild, even with proper protection like licensing and money to go to court if needed is sucked up by a mass of web scrapers, bots, and patent trolls in pursuit of rent seeking or to not fall behind others doing the same.

I think some guy at a large company said it best: "Whoever is left standing with the biggest and baddest AI model will find the ‘One Piece’ and be king of the AI startups.”

Then they killed him on a big podium. I don’t remember the details, please don’t sue me.

I am not sure why anyone would want to train their AI using my code, or similar code, but I’m sure it has happened. I envision some newbie prompting for the answer to a niche question for ChatGippity to respond with a solution similar to mine, with an O(n) that will continue looping until the eventual heat death of the universe. Not because I want to sabotage anyone, but as I am learning, the first draft of my code is often “junk,” and I don’t go around claiming perfection or publishing it for a reason.

All jokes aside, I’m sure our bad practice projects would have been filtered out. Right?

Illusion of Creation

It’s easier than ever to copy what prompts out of an LLM and spam it across the web. Unless you have decent prompts and context to mask it, the output is obvious, giving its signature robot vibes from the “average” of every shared opinion, research paper, and project the model was trained on.

It feels soulless because it lacks applied thinking and reflection by the user.

Many professions now require you to compete with those who fully embrace AI at scale. Driving this is a subtle but ever-present gravitational pull toward the path of least effort for what is perceived as the most output possible. With so much desire for a product that makes thinking “automated,” retail AI products may continue to improve far into the future, assuming they find a way to turn a profit that is.

The path of least resistance is not a suggestion, it’s a law of nature, and while AI is not objectively bad, the way in which it is used by some is not good to say the least.

Access to information and automation are some of the main pros of modern society, and I’m sure you already know the obvious arguments against AI. The world has already proven that ethical, moral, and environmental concerns will not stop people from doing what they want, unfortunately. It’s the cliché irony in having advanced tools, but being too senseless to wield them safely, which seems to be a trademark of human nature.

What I am worried about instead is the death of experts. Not the generic gatekeeping and annoying jerk behind a phoney degree or nepo-executive position “expert.” I’m talking about people who truly love their craft, hyper-fixate on learning, solve complex problems, and have the desire to pass this knowledge forward. The type of mentor I wish I had but never did.

Stuff that may never be found in an AI training dataset, or even be excluded, because it’s a deep experience that matters only from the perspective of an original thinker who does not post garbage on X, Reddit, and LinkedIn all day.

Who gets to decide what is “quality” or “ethical” data? If I use wording a scraper doesn’t agree with, even if I’m right, does that mean it's not worthy?

Don’t get me wrong, smart people will find ways to use AI as a tool and enhance whatever it is they are doing and still learn the material because they don’t really have a choice, for the time being. However, I am afraid that for many in the coming years, they may also loose the desire to “get good” at a craft they otherwise would have, even just as a hobby.

True or not, some believe it's not worth it, because why bother if a robot can do it good enough?

Illusion of Ownership

The generic “Oh no! People will have no brain now” argument is a recurring theme. The same thing was said about search engines, and how Google is a monopoly on access to said information. The fear was the same, people will “Google” the info they need and never “think” again.

In the case of search engines, however, you are forced to click on a link that takes you to a website where the alleged person who made the content (at least in theory) could get credit for what they did. Or if you have resources, go through some publishing process to help ensure your name is stamped on said thing or patented before you release it into the wild.

This feeble attempt at ownership is what drives much of (fortunately not all) the incentive to create and share value.

It’s far from perfect, pirating and plagiarism dilute the idea of owning “digital media,” but it works well enough that people still make software, write books, create content, and provide services online all the time.

Could AI simply output these sources for the given data after every prompt? This sounds great, but in reality, when it does give sources, even when you ask for it, links and citations are often poor quality such as a Reddit post not fully related to the prompt, or even flat-out broken and lead nowhere.

While I know this will improve over time and can be enhanced right now with proper prompts and specialized agents, etc, it leaves me with more questions than answers:

  • Who ultimately decides what “sources” to use and train models on?
  • Which of the possibly millions of sources that were used will be shown to the user when they ask for it and why?
  • Will there be a priority system for the sources, for example, "this source matters more and why," and who gets to decide that? Is it a poll or another AI using some guided metric, and who gets to decide that as well?
  • Similar to search engine results, how much can LLM output be biased in favor of a specific narrative the model creators want?
  • Can you patent what comes out of an LLM without using sources? What happens if major countries just decide one day you cant, what happens to all the FOSS vibe coded projects?
  • Can you even 100% prove or disprove a project was AI coded? Who gets the final verdict on if a project was done with AI or not?
  • Will the "amount" of AI that is or is not used decided if a project is worthy or not for copyright and licensing? Is it a spectrum or some subjective metric like tokens used to lines of code or something?
  • How do you even know an AI is lying when the question is complex? This assumes you know more than or have easy access to alternative information that is validated. Again, similar to Googling something with the intention of finding the result you want to find and confirming your bias.
  • If we need more regulations on how AI is used or trained for some unknown reason in the future, who is going to stop people from using it? ChatGippity is nearly synonymous with “Google it” for many now, and I have a sneaking suspicion that “guardrails” on AI will not stop those who understand how to leverage it from doing whatever they want if they have the hardware to run it locally. In just the same way hackers do whatever they want today with enough skill and resources....

Illusion of Power

Imagine using an LLM to make a new product or piece of content, do you really want to see a list of all the sources and all related patents for every prompt?

The power to generate something fast comes with responsibility. Seeing the sources would mean your history of interacting with the LLM could hold you accountable for plagiarism or patent infringement, even if unintentional.

And yes, unless you run it locally or it explicitly says otherwise, everything you prompt an LLM with is potentially saved long-term. It is not held to the same security standard as other systems are, like a bank or medical data for example, at least not yet. So if you freely give up your medical info, PII, or evidence for a crime, that’s on you.

Most who say otherwise, including the ones letting you use their robot, are lying to you.

Imagine you do something illegal, or someone falsely accuses you of doing something illegal and you go to court, the court may try to query for it, and it will become public information if ruled. This has already happened, and I doubt it will be the last time.

Even if not publicly available, its clear someone will have access to the data and know exactly who prompted it. It’s too much like giving a chainsaw to a child for a government to ignore. People just willingly enter their deepest and darkest thoughts they would never share otherwise, as if it were their therapist.

When something is free or cheap, to the point the worlds largest companies are willingly spending billions of dollars without an obvious way to make a return yet, you have to question why. It's just how the world works now. Anyone in the space with a brain knows, the last ones standing with the best data also have the most power.

They will not be held accountable in the same way you or I would be when they are caught doing something wrong to get that data. Especially if the dang thing is so deeply integrated into society and infrastructure that no one could afford do anything about it anyways...

"Oh, and I suppose a great muggle like yourself's gonna stop him, are ya?"

The idea that you can ask whatever you want from a free or cheap LLM “secretly” and you can do whatever you want will always be an illusion. The same goes for anything on the internet. You do not own the AI or the data center, some corporation does. If you really want this “power” for yourself, you have to build the infrastructure yourself and run it locally.

Companies selling you a subscription to use their pet robot for a monthly fee do not want the credit to go anywhere else, because then their pet robot is subject to copyright laws. Just like any business, investors don’t want heavy competition, or for their product to be subjected to complexity, they want a super deep moat.

Alpaca Giraffe Iguana

I’m not entirely against AI, see the image for this post above. I generated a “funny” image to make a point because its obvious I didn’t make it, then I put some bait text on it in GIMP to really push the low-effort slop look.

Most AI systems, especially LLMs, are just over-hyped and power hungry tools attached to fancy webscrapers running in a data center, I don’t really care.

What I am not a huge fan of are the motivations of the people who trained and host the tools. They present it as a replacement for things that make no sense, use scare tactics to manipulate, and steal data because it boosts their “stonks” for shareholders in hopes it will make more money.

As an investor myself, it's insulting.

The only thing that saddens me more than misaligned incentive and manipulation are the people who say, “You don’t even understand how AI works bro” any time someone has an argument to the contrary. As someone who has some real world experience using it, I can assure you that the majority of people who claim they understand, in fact, do not. That does not mean they are “idiots,” people can still get the gist. Those incentivized by returns, not by improving humanity like they claim, may not actually care what happens to you while using their product.

That does not just apply to AI.

At my previous job, I was forced to use AI for tasks it should not have been, or we would be punished as a team. It was used to write garbage code, the review process was virtually non-existent, and the only thing that mattered was velocity so management could lock in a bonus.

It didn’t start out that way, I was praying it would go back to normal after a while, but it just kept getting worse, and I guess I am a masochist.

Yet I still forced the code to work, I was one of the people at my company to fully adopt it into my workflow despite pushing back originally. They even used some of my ideas and never gave me credit for it. Even then, that would have been fine as long as the job hadn’t turned into a sleepless 80+ hours a week sh** show.

Getting past that, people were starting to not understand how the system was working at a fundamental level and disregard security measures for the sake of speed. I understand this can happen in larger code bases as complexity grows, but I mean the system itself was running in a way we would eventually have no meaningful control over, but still be expected to force it to work.

This was in the best interest of the Product Owners at the time, if the developers and QA had no meaningful say over what was or wasn’t working, even when it clearly wasn’t, they could just force it to production and then have people like myself and the offshore team (if you know you know) to fix it over the weekend without spending more time on paper. This way they could still claim AI and poor decisions were still making the process faster. It's easy to blame the developers for not being “good enough” with AI, while in reality your product breaks because you agreed to short deadlines without asking what it would really take to build it properly.

With or without AI, it still has to go through QA and UA, often needing additional tweaks to make end users happy.

If you don’t work in software, I mean doing full feature sets that take 50+ people with no sleep in 1 to 2 week blocks, them do it again on repeat without real breaks until you retire.

If I had some control over whatever systems we were adopting and handled the configuration, I could have at least tried, but due to bureaucracy, I was not allowed to try, I had no real power. When I brought up this concern to my manager, the only person who had any leverage to push back and cared, he told me he planned not to work there anymore once that became the norm.

Notice he did not say if. He was giving me a hint under that table. He planned to get his equity, then get out, and that I should to.

I hope it's clear that AI didn’t cause the issue, people did.

And for the record, I am not able to get behind the “quiet quitting” BS and I deeply cared about the quality of what we were doing, so I quit. Despite manipulative attempts to keep me there in the past, one going as far as threatening to blackball me.

“You are a talented engineer, I would hate to see your career ruined.”

This is what illusions of fear, money, and promise of power will do to people. It drives them crazy, and they say sh** they don’t mean and probably regret. This individual is lucky I didn’t go to HR, but I know they're not actually a bad person, they just wanted to retire soon.

I was also so tired, I kinda just sat there tuning it out, to be honest. In tandem with the job, this was a low period in my life.

Anyways, it felt easier to do my job in the beginning, and I felt the urge to keep using AI, kinda like a drug. For example, as someone with dyslexia, I use Grammarly often, but the urge to use AI for all my grammar and spelling needs and get the output quickly without thinking is so strong, it's painful.

If you have a powerful enough PC, you can run a model that’s “good enough” for basic spelling and grammar locally. You don’t need a ChatGippity Yes Man subscription or a Grok Nudity Generator to do it. You don’t even have to know what you are doing to run small models for simple tasks on your PC. While expensive, all you need is a good GPU, enough RAM, Docker, the right commands, and finally not be a moron after watching a video or two on configuration.

It’s not just LLMs. There are other applications for AI that are not often talked about in the news, and nobody knows what the real limitations and consequences will be. So much so, they can’t get people like Alex Karp to shut up about how “great” it is.

Oddly enough, I actually like Alex's enthusiasm, I would kill to be enthusiastic for AI, but I'm just not.

To wrap this up, I doubt super intelligence is on the radar anytime soon, if ever. I also doubt AI will be a mass drag and drop replacement for people in the way it is advertised in the near future either. But maybe it doesn’t need to be, if it's “good enough,” do you think the model itself, the company that created it, or a government wielding it will care about you, your prompts, your SaaS project, your job, or anything at all really?

If anyone could just click some buttons to deploy a billion dollar business, why would any rational organization just give that away? At the point of “super tool,” why would anyone stop at just exploiting profit? If that happens you have a lot more to worry about.

Will people even care until it directly impacts them several years from now? I’m not sure I truly care deep down anymore either.
