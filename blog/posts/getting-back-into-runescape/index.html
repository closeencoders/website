Old Dog, New Tricks: How RuneScape Stays Relevant in a Modern Era

Whether RuneScape is truly charming or just nostalgia is hard to say, but its recent updates, RuneLite plugins, and popularity speak for themselves.

By Josh - Apr 24, 2026 | posts | gaming, mmo

RuneScape. An ancient MMO and a massive part of my childhood, right up there with Halo 3 and Pokémon. I’m not alone in this feeling either, I have found this to be common among nerdy people roughly my age. But why would anyone be playing this game in 2026?

On the surface RuneScape looks and feels basic, and it’s difficult to pin down exactly what gives it charm beyond nostalgia through rose-colored glasses. Yet, the game still attracts new players and maintains an active community. It even appears to be growing, and there are some possible reasons why it has made a comeback.

For one, several popular streamers gave RuneScape a try. Because of this, a broader audience has become aware of the game with the desire to try it. While the streams have died down on Twitch, RuneScape’s popularity for content creation on YouTube is still there.

The sudden interest may have given the game the second wind that it needed. Many players who had been there since the mid-2000s didn’t enjoy what RuneScape had become. Upon the re-release of the 2007 version of RuneScape, or Old School RuneScape (OSRS), players were funneled back in time and had to start over from level three. Many people had already quit by that point, but others have returned due to nostalgia and the recent surge in popularity.

Another point, RuneScape's development team, under a company known as Jagex, have been on a winning streak for the past few years with new content and expanding the game to mobile and Steam. Players feel they have a say in the game’s direction through polls, barring a few exceptions. Along with solid development, alternative clients and launchers like RuneLite and Bolt have become popular and well-supported, resulting in the game being more accessible to a larger audience thanks to helpful plugins and quality-of-life improvements.

Lastly, RuneScape has a reward system and game loop that tilts toward delayed gratification and horizontal progression. At least within most aspects of OSRS, its not perfect, but when you achieve something, you can come back later (often years), and it still matters. This is a stark contrast to many modern games, or even just day-to-day activities in the modern world, sadly. Reaching a max level, let alone a maxed account, can take an extremely long time, but the game never forces you to max out just to have a good time, either.

A Second Wind

Knowing a bit of history helps with understanding the split between RuneScape, previously RuneScape 3 (RS3), and OSRS. It also explains why OSRS was in the right place at the right time in the past few years.

In roughly 1999, Andrew Gower created a prototype game called DeviousMUDand founded Jagex. This eventually evolved into the browser-based Java version of the original RuneScape in January 2001.

Yes, you read that right. RuneScape’s origin story is from a previous century and has exceeded just about all expectations for the lifespan of an MMO of its kind.

It just refuses to die despite some controversy. Over a decade after the game's inception, in November 2012, Jagex released the Evolution of Combat (EoC), which nearly killed the game, a demise most games meet after enough time passes and the executives behind the game lose touch with reality. The update replaced traditional mechanics with an ability-bar system that completely changed how the game dynamics worked.

It’s not that the update was exceedingly bad or anything, and this new version of the game gained it's own following, but it went against what players had come to expect of RuneScape. It was off-brand to say the least.

The community backlash was severe enough that Jagex ran a poll which voted to restore a 2007 version of the game only months later in 2013, creating the split between RS3 and OSRS that still exists today. This followed an earlier era of unrest that had started in late 2007, when the removal of Free Trade and the Wilderness PvP zone led to massive player protests until their eventual return.

The game lived on, but had taken some damage.

These two versions of the game offer vastly different experiences. RS3 was often criticized for its “gambling” feel due to microtransactions like Treasure Hunter (much of which was removed thankfully), whereas OSRS remains more focused on the original grind.

Fast-forward to the present, and OSRS has been hit with a wave of streamers and nostalgia-fueled returning players just as it was gaining traction from solid updates and development. This was partly due to World of Warcraft having frustrated a portion of its own player base, many stating they were fed up with the treadmill and the feeling that their previous achievements had no meaning after enough time passed.

Still Developed and Supported

You might think with the age of the game that support and updates would be limited, but to my own surprise, the Jagex dev team still pushes relevant updates and asks the community what they want through polls for new features and tweaks.

The most recent large scale change is the addition of the Sailing skill. Another is the addition to the Fletching skill, a minigame called Vale Totems, which included some training methods involving spirit animals. It’s ‘trippy’ and provides a nice change-up from other various bank-standing ways to train Fletching.

If you have been gone a very long time, you can also now play the game through Steam or on mobile for both OSRS and RuneScape. Playing on mobile is not perfect, but Jagex did a pretty good job adapting it, and it’s very playable.

Customization and Recommendations

There is no "best" or "right" way to play any game, especially RuneScape. Everyone plays the game differently, and that’s 100% okay.

However, for those who don’t know anything about the game, the average person may get a bad taste in their mouth in the early stages because some aspects of the game are not obvious. This is magnified for younger players, the older-looking UI and not knowing you can change or customize it pushes people away fast.

Even if they manage to look past the UI, there is often confusion about what to do after leaving the tutorial island. The intro does a decent job of showing you some mechanics, but it does not give you a major set of goals or direction. While this is one of the biggest pros of the game (aka freedom) it may not feel like a good thing to someone coming from other games or just a random person trying it for the first time.

If the player is still logged in after that, and forges their own path, if they pick a ‘cringy’ starting quest or try to train a slower skill like Hunter without using something like the wiki, runehq, or at least some videos to show them where to go, it’s easy to assume the entire game will be this tedious.

As a veteran, this is a cakewalk, but without help or guidance from somebody who has played before to aid you, you may have to navigate several days or so to see if it’s actually fun.

If you have patience and have played other MMOs, you may be more willing to push through, knowing it will get better and learning something “blind” is more rewarding. I would argue that many don’t feel this way, though. Especially if they subject themselves to free-to-play worlds killing tons of chickens or cows in a horde of bots for longer than necessary, only to be slapped in the face with anti-bot Grand Exchange (GE) restrictions or a weird quest requirement.

The initial no-reward game loop can feel awful, but it doesn’t have to.

So if you are impatient, then I am only proposing ways to improve the experience that you could choose from if they sound good to you.

Modern UI and A Different Client Like RuneLite

One of, if not the most important thing to know is that playing OSRS with the default client instead of something like RuneLite can be a major handicap. Unless you specifically want a “raw” old-school vibe, it won’t be as enjoyable for most people.

Once installed, use the drop-down menu to select the RuneLite client in your launcher. I recommend watching a video or two to see how to customize it to your liking and make the experience less annoying.

The main benefits are the plugins, everything from graphics improvements and item highlighting to quality-of-life perks. The list of improvements feels endless and can make the game feel like it belongs in the current decade.

At a minimum, I recommend doing the following:

  • If you don’t like the default UI, then change the UI to the modern layout and enable scaling/resizing in settings. This pairs well with RuneLite and other visual plugins.
  • If you hate the default graphics, then Install 117 HD Plugin.
  • If you are extra impatient and hate wikis, then install Quest Helper Plugin.
  • RuneLite might have automatic screenshots for achievements/levels on by default, if you hate that, you can disable it.
  • I talk about it in more depth later, but don't avoid the NPC Swap plugin and keybindings settings if you care about your sanity. The default is often... not great.

Do Easy Quests For Experience Early On

If you plan to do quests, consider starting them as soon as possible. Especially if they reward experience in skills you don’t like, but need to raise your level.

A great example would be the Museum Quiz in Varrock. It’s kind of quirky, but it’s short and gives good experience (if you are a lower level) for the Hunter and Slayer skills. You can then dodge the early-level grind for those skills, as they become much, MUCH better after level 10 to 20+.

If you wait too long and power level your skills, the amount of experience you get from the quests at a higher level does not feel worth the time.

A lot of the starting quests and miniquests, including the free-to-play quests, will boost your stats without you having to do the early grind from level one in many different skills. Going right into Hunter, Agility, or Runecrafting without some quests can feel like getting punched in the face.

The game doesn’t have to feel that way, and doing a few quests alleviates the early grind if you want it to.

But hey, maybe you will love starting Hunter from level one, and it will be your favorite skill, who am I to say otherwise?

Playing as an Ironman as Your First Character

If you come from other MMOs that have a decent grind, this may not apply to you, and you may feel right at home in Ironman Mode .

Playing in Ironman Mode is fun, but it limits player-to-player interaction. If you don’t want to play solo, meaning you literally cannot trade at all, it can be a terrible idea for a first character. Ironman Mode in RuneScape is a bit different than other games, there is Ultimate Ironman, Hardcore Ironman, and Ironman.

"If a Hardcore Ironman dies an unsafe death, they will be converted to an Ironman"

It's not identical to World of Warcraft, so consider reading more about it to avoid wasting your time.

Once you understand the game better, it becomes a fun challenge and a flex, but as a newbie, it can feel isolating. It makes many of the skills and quests go from hundreds of hours to thousands to achieve completion for a normal person with a life.

If you absolutely feel the need, at the very least make sure you know what you are getting yourself into a bit and you like to play more solo. If not, consider group Ironman mode with friends. Most will burn out or regret the decision when you hit a quest requirement that needs an item you can't buy and is only attainable from some crazy niche grind.

Keybindings and Shift-click Drop Items

OSRS has lots of options for keybindings and misc settings. That paired with RuneLite plugins like "NPC Swap" lets you swap specific click options on NPCs. This is huge for training skills like thieving.

Another common complaint I hear a lot is the inventory management, which I completely agree with. To alleviate this, there is also a setting that lets you drop items more quickly using shift. It’s not dependent on RuneLite, it’s built into the game.

In settings, under controls, scroll down and check “Shift click drop items.” Now you can quickly empty your inventory if you need to by holding down Shift and spamming clicks on items in your inventory.

I was a little upset when I found out this was a thing. I had been right-clicking for a few days and wondering how people were dropping their entire inventory in a few seconds or less.

Just… Uhhhh!

You Can Organize Your Bank

New players often don't notice you can organize your bank, down to an excruciating level of detail if desired. Making new tabs and dragging and dropping items in the manner you want in your bank makes the experience of collecting items for quests and tasks less frustrating.

There are also plugins that will help automate or improve your bank as well if using RuneLite. My bank is not super organized at the moment, but even a little bit helps in the long run.

If You Play On Linux

The only reasons I logout of Debian and launch Microsoft Windows on my dual-booted machine is for gaming and work. But you can now play OSRS on Linux with ease. Granted, you may need to use a third-party launcher if you are not inclined to tinker.

I am currently using the Bolt launcher with RuneLite through Flatpak. I know third-party launchers and clients feel sketchy, so recommend reading the source code. I believe the Bolt open source code migrated from GitHub to Codeberg and I am afraid to link it directly, I don't want to lead people to the wrong place.

I know that some hate Flatpak, but that is one of the easiest ways to get the game up and going, otherwise play around with Wine and graphics settings. Wine has become much better over the years, but I don’t prefer Wine for RuneScape.

Ignore The Dark Side

I alluded to it in the history section, but OSRS has its own skeletons in the closet. Even mentioning them can get you a bit flamed since part of the community is strongly against it, or in the "click" if you catch my drift.

Like most MMOs, RuneScape deals with bots and spam marketing. What’s less discussed is the aging player base, and which products are motivated to target that demographic.

I hope I don’t need to be more blunt than that, but on free-to-play worlds, you can’t really avoid it. My suggestion is use your "add to ignore" list and don't be afraid to report when you need to.

The majority of the community is actually really nice and often goes out of its way to help new players.

I have noticed new players often don't know you can even report, which also adds that person to the ignore list. Or you can just add that player to the ignore list.

Just right click the player's name and select the option.

Neither of these events alerts the other player, other than that you won't respond anymore, of course.

Game Loop

As far as MMOs go, OSRS is a master class at horizontal progression and respecting the ‘grind’.

Because of this, when you earn something in OSRS, it often carries real meaning and weight, and it will stay that way years into the future. When you complete that quest, unlock a new area or a nifty piece of armor, you feel it. Then, when you login six months later, it's still there and most likely just as useful as the day you unlocked it.

There isn’t a class system either, and you can do just about anything you want whenever you want, barring level and quest requirements for obvious things.

Want to make an account, then grind only cooking to 99 for no reason, and just walk around making jokes? You can.

Want to be some kind of pure or PVP build, never do more than a few quests, and go around trying to kill people and bots in the wilderness and PVP worlds just to post it on YouTube for fun? Go for it, It can be quite profitable too.

There is no main quest line that is required to play the game. For example, many players make accounts called “skillers” with only the intention of leveling up non-combat skills because they find it therapeutic.

You can play however you want.

Final Thoughts

RuneScape is not a perfect game, and it's not for everyone. This can be said for any game, but I think it does have a weird charm to it, and is worth a try if you enjoy sandbox-style MMOs. Everything from its art style to audio is iconic, and its progression system sets it apart from most games in the genre. If the game feels too basic, just remember to give RuneLite a try, there may be a plugin that fixes an issue you can't stand about the game.
